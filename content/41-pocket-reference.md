# The pocket reference

You made it to the last chapter, fully charged. Here's the whole course
compressed into one page you can come back to forever.

## The units

| Unit | Symbol | Measures | Kitchen translation |
| ---- | ------ | -------- | ------------------- |
| **Volt** | V | Voltage — the push | Water pressure in the pipe |
| **Amp** | A | Current — the flow | Litres per second through it |
| **Ohm** | Ω | Resistance | How pinched the hose is |
| **Watt** | W | Power — work per second | Pressure × flow, the action |
| **Kilowatt-hour** | kWh | Energy over time | What the bill actually counts |
| **Hertz** | Hz | AC cycles per second | The grid's heartbeat (50 in Europe) |

## The two laws that run everything

```
current = voltage ÷ resistance      (I = V / R)   — Ohm's law
power   = voltage × current         (P = V × I)   — the watt equation
```

## Numbers worth keeping

- **230 V** — European wall outlet. Respect it.
- **400,000 V** — the big pylons, so the amps (and heat losses) stay tiny.
- **50 Hz** — every generator on the European grid, spinning in lockstep.
- **~0.03 A** — enough current through a body to be lethal; also exactly
  where your RCD draws its line.
- **2,000 W** — a kettle. **100 W** — a fridge. **20 W** — a phone charger.
- **< 1 mm/s** — actual electron drift speed. The *push* travels near light
  speed; the electrons commute like snails.

## The big ideas, one line each

1. Electricity is a push (voltage) moving a crowd of charge (current) around
   an unbroken loop (circuit).
2. Nearly every power plant is a spinning magnet; only solar skips the spin.
3. The grid stores almost nothing — supply matches demand every second, and
   the frequency is the live pulse of that balance.
4. Transformers step voltage up to travel cheap and down to arrive safe.
5. Your home's guardians: breakers protect the wires, earth protects the
   appliances, the RCD protects *you*.
6. Current does the damage; voltage decides whether it can flow; water
   removes your armour.
7. Batteries store chemistry, mountains store water, and storage is the key
   that unlocks a weather-powered grid.

## The final exam (self-graded, gentle, one question per chapter)

1. When you flip a switch, what actually travels at near light speed —
   the electrons, or something else?
2. Which unit is the "pressure" and which is the "flow"?
3. Why did one dead bulb kill the *entire* string of old fairy lights?
4. What do a nuclear plant and an ordinary kettle have in common?
5. Why does the grid ship power at 400,000 volts instead of 230?
6. Which device in your hallway notices 0.03 missing amps and saves your
   life — and what's the button on it for?
7. True or false: rubber sneakers make outlet mishaps fine.
8. Where does most of the world's stored electricity sit right now?
