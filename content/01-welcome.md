# Welcome to the current

Right now, as you read this, an invisible force is lighting your screen,
cooling your fridge, charging your phone, and keeping several billion other
machines alive — and most of us have absolutely no idea how it works.

That ends today. This is a short, fun course about **electricity**: what it
actually is, how it's made, how it sneaks into your wall, and why you can
trust it with your life while it simultaneously deserves your full respect.

No physics degree required. If you've ever successfully plugged in a toaster,
you're qualified.

## Why bother understanding it?

Because electricity is the closest thing we have to actual magic, and you're
*paying for it monthly* without knowing what "it" is:

- Every power plant on Earth — coal, nuclear, hydro, wind — is secretly the
  **same machine**: something spinning a magnet. (Solar is the one weird
  exception. We'll get there.)
- The electrical grid is often called the **largest machine ever built** —
  a single connected contraption spanning entire continents, which must
  balance itself perfectly *every second* or fall over.
- The electrons in your charging cable move slower than a snail. Seriously.
  What moves fast is something far stranger.
- Your home contains multiple devices that exist purely to save your life,
  installed by law, waiting silently for the one millisecond they're needed.

> **⚡ Spark fact:** Each chapter of this course ends up somewhere around a
> five-minute read — roughly the time it takes to boil water for one cup of
> tea, using about 0.02 kWh, which at typical prices costs less than the
> smallest coin you own. Electricity is absurdly cheap for what it does.
> You'll never look at a kettle the same way again.

## What you'll learn

The course runs in two arcs. **The big world** first:

- What electricity *actually is* (hint: not "electrons zooming through
  wires").
- Fluent **volts, amps, ohms and watts** — and an electricity bill you can
  read with your head held high.
- The journey of a watt: spinning turbine → 400,000-volt pylons →
  transformer → your phone charger.
- Your fuse box, decoded — and why electricity hurts, minus the myths.

Then **down to the electron scale**, onto the workbench:

- How instruments make the invisible measurable — and why in-circuit
  readings lie.
- What resistors, capacitors, diodes, transistors and MOSFETs really do,
  and why every LED travels with a bodyguard.
- The metallurgy of connections: what a solder joint *is*, what flux does,
  why crimps outlive solder.
- What a microcontroller pin is behind the metal — logic levels, PWM, and
  honest power budgets.
- How chips converse (UART, I2C, SPI), how the analog world gets digitised
  — and why every LED-strip wiring rule is derivable physics, not folklore.
